# RFC-0038 — Living Innovation Lab (iLab)

## Abstract

This document specifies the Living Innovation Lab (iLab) as the
infrastructure of epistemic resistance: a physical-and-social adaptive
space where un-captured nodes — brokers, central connectors, energizers,
and external participants — exchange knowledge outside formal channels.
The iLab is anti-theater by construction: it removes obstacles between
people and the coffee machine, it never prescribes the second step, and
it formalizes nothing and no one (Non-Capture Principle).

> **Dual-Layer Notice**: This RFC presents the Living Innovation Lab as a
> satirical vehicle for the book's constructive complement. The serious
> layer is structural: institutions that want candid knowledge flow must
> maintain a space the hierarchy does not control. The satirical layer is
> the proposal's own form: the iLab reads like a facility with rooms,
> programs, and participants — the same institutional grammar that, in
> the book, produces Decision Theater. The distinction is that the iLab
> is a *clearing*, not a *program*: it removes walls instead of building
> them.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Problem Statement

The book diagnoses how organizations destroy knowledge: fragmentation
(RFC-0003), gravity (RFC-0005), capture (RFC-0035), committee entropy
(RFC-0014), and amnesia (RFC-0007). The diagnosis is complete; the
constructive complement is not. A reader who finishes the book can name
the failure modes but cannot name a single institutional form that
resists them.

This RFC names one such form. It does not claim to be the only one, or
the first. It claims to be coherent with the mechanisms the book
describes — the same way an antidote is coherent with a poison.

### 1.2 The Author's Relationship to the Source

The iLab vision was drafted by the author of this RFC as a university
open-innovation proposal (UPdigital, University of Porto). That draft was
never published. This RFC adapts the vision into the OEP canon as an
informational description of an infrastructure, not as a plan to be
rolled out. Consistent with Caminho A, the draft is not cited: only
published sources are citable in canon.

### 1.3 Scope

This RFC is **Informational**. It describes what an iLab is, why the
canon's mechanisms require it, and what it must refuse to become. It
does not specify how to launch one, measure one, or staff one. Any
section that reads as a rollout procedure is a misreading; the RFC
actively resists that reading (Section 7).

## 3. The Anti-Theater

### 3.1 Theater as the Inverse of Knowledge

The canon's theater RFCs share one structure: a performance stands in for
a function. Decision Theater (RFC-0004) stages decision-making without
deciding; Consensus Theater (RFC-0014) stages agreement without
agreement; Theater of Sharing (RFC-0009) stages knowledge-sharing while
the incentive structure keeps knowledge hoarded.

The iLab inverts this. Where theater produces the *appearance* of the
function, the iLab produces the function without the appearance. There
is no stage, no agenda, no dashboard. There is a space, a time, and
people who are allowed to talk to each other without routing the
conversation through a committee.

### 3.2 Anti-Theater Properties

| Property | Theater (failure) | iLab (anti-theater) |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Space | A room built for the performance | A place that already exists — a floor, a coffee area, a shared bench — left alone |
| Agenda | An agenda item | None. People arrive and talk |
| Output | A decision, a vote, a slide deck | A conversation that changes what someone knows |
| Attendance | Mandated | Voluntary, and low-attendance weeks are normal |
| Metric | Participation, approval, velocity | None (RFC-0035 §6.3) |

The right column is deliberately sparse. Every property the institution
adds to the right column converts the iLab into the left column
(Non-Capture Principle; the Vetted Space failure mode, RFC-0035 §6.2).

## 4. Anatomy of the Space

### 4.1 An Adaptive Space

The iLab is an Adaptive Space (TERMINOLOGY; Arena et al. 2017): the
informal context in which non-prescriptive collaboration occurs and
connectors operate. It is not a building, a program, or a department. It
is the permission structure that already exists in every organization —
the place where work really gets decided — given enough protection to
survive formalization pressure.

### 4.2 The Three Network Roles

The iLab's ecology is the balance of the three connector archetypes from
RFC-0035 §3.3.1 (Arena, Cross, Sims, & Uhl-Bien 2017):

- **Brokers** bridge clusters that otherwise never meet. In the iLab, a
  broker is someone who introduces the finance team's question to the
  data team's half-formed idea.
- **Central Connectors** anchor one cluster: "ask them" is said about
  them inside the cluster.
- **Energizers** are position-independent; projects and volunteers form
  around them.

The iLab does not create these roles. They exist wherever informal
networks exist. The iLab's only contribution is to let the roles operate
without capture — no title, no badge, no promotion (Rule ESP-6). The
Competence Tax (TERMINOLOGY; RFC-0035 §6.2) is the failure mode the iLab
most directly opposes: the Energizer overloaded with administrative work
"because they get things done." In the iLab, the Energizer is left to
energize.

The iLab is also the answer to the Inverted Pyramid (TERMINOLOGY): the
formal layer fields teams of "me alone" — one holder
per competency, no redundancy — while the adaptive space builds multiple
holders and multiple paths for each knowledge flow. Where the formal
layer is a pyramid of stakes, the iLab is the load-bearing training
ground: it is how the institution trains the several soldiers it will
need before one of them leaves.

### 4.3 External Un-Captured Nodes

Institutions differ in what external participants they can reach. A
university has students. An institution without university ties has
other sources: **interns, volunteers, community contributors, retirees,
or professionals in adjacent organizations**. The property that matters
is not the label — it is the capture profile:

| External node | Capture profile |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Volunteer | Un-captured by construction: self-selected, no career stake |
| Student | Un-captured by absence of employment stake; time-limited |
| Intern | Un-captured only if participation is decoupled from hiring evaluation; otherwise a formal channel (RFC-0035 §3.3.1 capture failure) |
| Retiree / former employee | Un-captured by removal from the promotion structure; carrier of institutional memory (RFC-0007 OAPP, RFC-0016 IMBP) |

The iLab does not need students. It needs *someone whose honesty is not
priced by the hierarchy*. Which institution offers that someone is a
local fact; the RFC's position is that external participants exist in
every institution's reach, and that the capture profile, not the label,
decides whether the channel stays open.

#### 4.3.1 The Verdict: External Nodes Extend RFC-0016, as a Live Layer

The external-node extension belongs to RFC-0016 (IMBP), not RFC-0007
(OAPP). RFC-0007 is prevention; its §1.3 explicitly excludes
backup/restore mechanics as RFC-0016's scope, and §1.4 calls RFC-0016
the last-resort restore. An external node's value is *holding* memory
outside the promotion structure — a redundancy function, not a
prevention function. Two conditions bind the extension:

1. **Flow, not tenure.** The Half-Day promises flow (RFC-0035 §3.3.2).
   Students graduate; volunteers leave without notice. The extension
   treats external nodes as a live, non-durable memory layer — current
   knowledge held while present — never a permanent archive replacing
   RFC-0016's durable internal backup (MBF semantics).
2. **Intern decoupling is a precondition, not a label property.**
   Interns are un-captured ONLY if participation is decoupled from
   hiring evaluation; otherwise the internship feeds a performance
   incentive and the intern is a captured node (RFC-0035 §3.3.1 capture
   failure). This is the Non-Capture Principle (Rule ESP-6) applied to
   the boundary class.

The four classes — volunteers, students, interns, retirees — stay
distinct. The intern is the boundary case that tests the concept's
limit; merging it would hide the conditional un-capture property.

### 4.4 Brokering as a Practice, Not a Platform

The paper that inspired this RFC describes a web platform that connects
project proposers to contributors. This RFC does not adopt the platform.
A formal brokering system is a formal channel: it routes requests,
assigns pairs, and tracks outcomes — the Vetted Space failure mode
(RFC-0035 §6.2). Brokering in the iLab is a *practice* performed by
Brokers, not a system run by the institution. The platform question is
settled here (§4.4.1): any formal platform fails the Non-Capture
Principle, so the iLab keeps the practice and rejects the platform.

#### 4.4.1 The Verdict: Why a Formal Platform Is Rejected

The Brokering System fails Non-Capture by three independent mechanisms,
each already a named failure mode:

1. **Routing is Vetted Space.** A platform that "connects project
   proposers with potential contributors" runs a matching process. The
   half-day becomes a proposal funnel; brokers submit instead of
   assemble (RFC-0035 §6.2).
2. **Tracking is a record.** A "portfolio of contributions" is a
   permanent, monitorable record of who contributed what — the
   Surveillance failure mode (RFC-0035 §6.2) and the Wiki Graveyard
   (RFC-0016 §4.4). ESP §6.3 forbids dashboards and reports; a portfolio
   is a dashboard wearing a contributor's name.
3. **A permanent channel is the least adaptive space.** The paper's own
   definition (iLab §2.1.4) says the adaptive space "is not a physical
   building or a laboratory *per se*" — it is fluid and adjusts to
   necessity. A web platform is the least fluid structure an institution
   can build: it fixes the space in place, versioned and archived, while
   the space's only property that matters is that it can be torn down
   and rebuilt by the people in it.

The function the platform wanted survives as practice: proposers meet
contributors because Brokers introduce them, and the redundancy that
keeps a project alive after its originator leaves is built by the
training ground (§4.2), not by a matching algorithm. Nothing the
platform promised is lost by rejecting it; everything it promised is
already served by the practice this RFC keeps.

#### 4.4.2 The Ephemeral Counter-Image

The physical post-it wall — the paper's own operational variant — is the
canonical image of what brokering infrastructure looks like when it does
not capture: it is visible, it is local, and it leaves no record. A
challenge is announced on the wall, a Broker reads it to someone who can
answer it, and the post-it falls when the problem is solved. There is no
dashboard, no archive, no outcome log (ESP §6.3). The wall is not a
platform; it is a prop for a practice.

## 5. Why It Does Not Capture

### 5.1 Non-Capture Principle

RFC-0035 Rule ESP-6: rewarding a connector into a position removes them
from the informal network that made them useful. The iLab applies the
same rule to the space itself:

- No badges, titles, or "Champion" programs (they convert the connector
  into a performance).
- No role formalization (a Broker converted into a "Coordinator" is no
  longer a Broker).
- No metrics, dashboards, or reports (RFC-0035 §6.3). The iLab cannot be
  measured because measuring it is the first act of capturing it.

### 5.2 The Institutional Temptation

Institutions will be tempted to "support" the iLab: fund it, staff it,
brand it, put it on the org chart. Each act of support is an act of
capture. The iLab survives only while it remains the thing the
institution does not manage. This is not a design flaw; it is the design
(Theorem-001: the channel's capacity depends on its informality).

### 5.3 Compensation Justice

The iLab formalizes nothing and no one — and the institution pays for the
value the space lets flow. The two sentences are the same doctrine, read
from both sides (Compensation Justice; RFC-0035 Rule ESP-6).

Non-Capture forbids converting the broker into a *position*: a title, a
reporting line, a badge. It never forbids paying the broker for the value
they already create — and refusing to pay is not Non-Capture, it is
exploitation wearing its costume. A broker rewarded only in ceremony —
thank-you notes, "champion" praise, a name in the annual report — is a
broker being harvested. The institution takes the knowledge flow and
returns a gesture. The failure mode has a name: **Recognition Theater**
(RFC-0035 §6.2). It is paired with the Competence Tax — the Energizer
overloaded "because they get things done" — as the two ways an
institution extracts value and returns gesture. Both close the channel:
one by burnout, one by betrayal.

The Dilbert Principle (Adams 1996) explains why the two moves are
distinct and why both are wrong. Adams observed that organizations
systematically promote the sub-competent into management, to limit the
damage they can do: management as containment, not reward. The corollary
is that management is where the organization sends what it cannot use.
Keeping the broker out of management is therefore not withholding a
prize — it is refusing to contain the broker's competence. And because
the institution refuses to contain it, it must pay for it. A broker who
is neither promoted nor paid has been handled the worst of both ways:
their competence used, their labor extracted, their value returned as a
speech.

The iLab's security model is therefore not "the institution ignores us
and we stay free". It is "the institution does not manage us, and pays us
fairly." The first half keeps the channel open; the second half keeps it
safe. An under-compensated broker is a least-trusting member, and the
Minimum Trust Law (RFC-0017 §3.9.4) says one such member degrades the
space for all.

## 6. Failure Modes the Lab Opposes

| Mode | Canon anchor | How the iLab opposes it |
|------|--------------|-------------------------|
| Knowledge Gravity | RFC-0005 | Provides a low-gravity zone where truth vertices and power vertices can meet without the power vertex converting the meeting into a reporting line |
| Committee Entropy | RFC-0014 | Replaces the committee as the venue for "what should we do" questions; the answer is a conversation, not a motion |
| Competence Tax | RFC-0035 §6.2 | Leaves Energizers to energize instead of loading them with administrative work |
| Dilbert Principle | Adams 1996; §5.3 | Keeps brokers out of management — where competence is contained, not rewarded — and therefore owes them fair payment for the value the space lets flow |
| Recognition Theater | RFC-0035 §6.2; §5.3 | Refuses to under-pay the brokers who carry the space; symbolic reward is not compensation, and a broker paid only in ceremony is a broker being harvested |
| Inverted Pyramid | TERMINOLOGY | Builds redundancy — multiple holders, multiple paths — where the formal layer fields "me alone" teams; the adaptive space is the load-bearing training ground |
| Theater of Sharing | RFC-0009 | Produces actual knowledge flow instead of the performance of it |
| Amnesia | RFC-0007, RFC-0016 | Brokers and external memory carriers (retirees, volunteers, students) hold memory the formal channel cannot hold |
| Vetted Space | RFC-0035 §6.2 | Refuses every added process (approval, dashboard, portfolio) as capture |

## 7. Boundaries — What This RFC Does Not Do

1. **It does not prescribe the second step.** The book's preface is
   explicit: "Do not implement the protocols... The second step is not in
   this book. The second step is by the coffee machine" (Preface). The
   iLab does not provide the second step. It provides the *coffee
   machine*: a place where the second step can happen without being
   routed through the hierarchy. The iLab is not the answer; it is the
   infrastructure that lets someone find the answer.
2. **It does not contain a rollout procedure.** No phases, no timeline,
   no milestones, no staffing plan. A reader who wants to "implement an
   iLab" is asked to re-read Section 3.2: the properties that make the
   space work are the ones that resist implementation.
3. **It does not create metrics.** RFC-0035 §6.3 applies unchanged.
4. **It does not create new protocols.** The protocol numbers following
   RFC-0035 remain unallocated; the iLab is a space, not a procedure.
5. **It does not formalize external participants.** The capture-profile
   taxonomy (Section 4.3) is observation, not enrollment. A "volunteer
   program" with an application process is a formal channel by another
   name.

## 9. Security Considerations

### 9.1 Capture by Support

The most likely attack on the iLab is well-intentioned: funding, naming,
staffing. Each is a formalization. Section 5.2 is the security model:
the iLab is secure precisely because the institution does not manage it.

### 9.2 Performance Substitution

A "living lab" that is in fact a program — with a leader, a budget line,
and quarterly goals — is Decision Theater wearing a lab coat. The
distinguishing test is the Vetted Space failure mode: does the space
still permit contact the hierarchy does not control?

### 9.3 Surveillance Misuse

Mapping who attends the iLab, who talks to whom, or which projects
emerge is the same surveillance failure RFC-0035 §5.1 describes. The
iLab is not observable by design; observing it closes it.

## Appendix B: Open Issues

1. **Formalization Threshold**: Where the line sits between "a space
   left alone" and "a facility the institution manages" is not
   quantifiable. The RFC treats this as a feature (measuring it would
   capture it), but the boundary case — a funded room that remains
   informal — is unresolved. [VERIFIED]

   *Resolution:* The threshold is not a funding axis; it is a control
   axis. The boundary case is not a stable third state — it resolves
   into one of two cases by the Vetted Space test (§9.2): does the
   space still permit contact the hierarchy does not control? Funding
   that prices *value* (room, coffee machine, refreshments) is not
   capture — compensation is not formalization (§5.3; Rule
   ESP-6). Funding that attaches to a *role* — a leader, a program
   name, a budget line with quarterly goals — is capture the moment it
   is attached, regardless of how informal the room's culture feels
   (§5.2, §9.2). The threshold therefore *cannot* be a metric: any
   measure of "how much formal is too much" is itself an act of
   management, and management is the capture it would quantify. The
   only falsifiable test is the §9.2 question, applied to the space,
   not to its funding line.

2. **External-Node Extension**: The RFC-0016 (IMBP) extension for
   external memory nodes is resolved as a live, non-durable memory
   layer (RFC-0007 is prevention, not restore). The RFC-0016
   amendment is ratified in RFC-0016 §9 (Amendments, 2026-08-07),
   pointing to §4.3.1 as the normative source. [VERIFIED]
